Functions

Functions are often the gateway to advanced Putnam strategies: look for substitution, domain restriction, symmetry, and functional equations.

  • Check if the problem suggests an additive or multiplicative identity.
  • Search for invariants under argument transformation.
  • Use substitution to reduce structure to polynomials or inequalities.

Matrices

Matrix-related problems often pivot through determinant identities, row operations, eigenvalues, and block decompositions.

  • Use row reduction carefully and track determinant sign changes.
  • Look for block structure that simplifies to smaller determinants.
  • Apply eigenvalue or trace concepts when the matrix is symmetric or has a special form.

Probability

Probability problems reward precise expectation calculations, conditioning, and random process intuition.

  • Translate the problem into states and transitions when possible.
  • Use linearity of expectation even if events are dependent.
  • Consider coupling, symmetry, and standard distributions for bounds.

Polynomials

Polynomials appear in many Putnam settings through root placement, factorization, and generating function techniques.

  • Look for behavior at large values and root constraints.
  • Use polynomial inequalities and factorization to control sign.
  • Apply generating functions to count coefficients or combinatorial quantities.
Return to main map

Game Theory

Game theory problems often reduce to invariants, strategic parity, and optimization of moves.

  • Model the problem as alternating turns or state changes.
  • Find invariant quantities that remain constant through moves.
  • Optimize by working backward from terminal positions.
